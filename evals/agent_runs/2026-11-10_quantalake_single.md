# Deal-Prep Briefing: QuantaLake

## Company snapshot
- QuantaLake is an open-source lakehouse project built on Apache Iceberg, with a commercial managed offering called **QuantaLake Cloud** [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Competitor Snapshot].
- The open-source edition is free and has a strong developer community with over 22,000 GitHub stars and an active Slack channel [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Strengths].
- QuantaLake Cloud pricing starts at $0.90 per compute-unit-hour [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Strengths].
- It is popular with engineering-led data teams in retail, e-commerce, and tech-forward mid-market accounts that prefer a "build, not buy" approach [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Competitor Snapshot].
- Primary target buyers are Heads of Data Engineering or Principal Engineers [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Competitor Snapshot].
- Self-hosted open-source deployments carry no security attestations and rely on community support, whereas SOC 2 Type II certification covers QuantaLake Cloud only [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Weaknesses / Our Strengths].
- QuantaLake is our toughest competitive matchup, with a head-to-head win rate of 49% (38 wins out of 78 deals) in H1 2026 [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Competitor Snapshot].

## Our relevant positioning
- **Native Iceberg Interoperability:** Since the NexaStream Core 2026.3 release (July 2026), Core natively reads and writes Apache Iceberg tables, eliminating QuantaLake's key value proposition around avoiding vendor lock-in [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Weaknesses / Our Strengths].
- **3-Year TCO Advantage:** A 3-year TCO benchmark for a mid-market retailer estimates QuantaLake Cloud plus separate BI tools and staffing at ~$1,020,000+ (driven by 2.5 FTE data engineers costing $1,350,000 and $150,000 in BI licenses), compared to NexaStream’s ~$640,000 [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Pricing / TCO Comparison (3-Year, Mid-Market Retailer)].
- **Built-in BI & Analytics:** Unlike QuantaLake, which lacks a native visualization layer, NexaStream includes Pulse dashboards and the Lumen AI assistant out of the box [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Weaknesses / Our Strengths].
- **Enterprise Governance & SLAs:** NexaStream holds platform-wide SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR, and HIPAA compliance, paired with a 1-hour P1 response SLA and dedicated CSM on Enterprise [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Weaknesses / Our Strengths].
- **Proven Competitive Win Strategy:** In the Lumos Retail Group deal, we defeated an internal QuantaLake POC by engaging the CFO in a joint TCO workshop to reveal open-source staffing costs while proving open-format capability with Iceberg support [casestudy_lumos_retail.md § Case Study: Lumos Retail Group > Why We Won].

## Talking points and opportunities
- **Expose hidden staffing overhead:** Ask how many engineers are dedicated to keeping the lakehouse running rather than delivering insights, noting that production QuantaLake setups typically require 2–4 dedicated data engineers ($360,000–$720,000/year in staffing) [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Weaknesses / Our Strengths] [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Landmines].
- **Question BI ownership and costs:** Ask which third-party BI tool will sit on top of QuantaLake and who will manage the integration, contrasting this with NexaStream’s included Pulse dashboards and Lumen AI assistant [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Weaknesses / Our Strengths] [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Landmines].
- **Neutralize lock-in objections:** Reassure technical buyers that NexaStream Core's native Apache Iceberg read/write capabilities allow them to retain open formats without running QuantaLake infrastructure [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Weaknesses / Our Strengths].
- **Probe compliance requirements:** Inquire whether internal auditors accept self-hosted QuantaLake open source given its lack of SOC 2 Type II attestations [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Weaknesses / Our Strengths] [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Landmines].
- **Offer a joint CFO TCO evaluation:** Propose a joint TCO workshop with executive leadership to contrast total software and staffing costs against QuantaLake, replicating the model used in the Lumos Retail evaluation [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Pricing / TCO Comparison (3-Year, Mid-Market Retailer)] [casestudy_lumos_retail.md § Case Study: Lumos Retail Group > Why We Won].

## Risks
- **Hardest head-to-head matchup:** Our win rate against QuantaLake is 49% (38 wins out of 78 deals in H1 2026), making it our most challenging competitive engagement [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Competitor Snapshot].
- **Strong engineering buyer bias:** Target buyers (Heads of Data Engineering or Principal Engineers) often reject commercial solutions under the assumption that internal engineering staff can maintain QuantaLake ("We already have engineers who can run it") [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Competitor Snapshot].
- **Low barrier to entry:** QuantaLake OSS is free with widespread community adoption (22,000+ GitHub stars), and QuantaLake Cloud offers an initial low entry rate of $0.90 per compute-unit-hour [battlecard_quantalake.md § Competitive Battle Card: QuantaLake > Their Strengths].
